Symposium Abstract / Resumen del simposio

In this transdisciplinary symposium we begin to think from the frictions that arise between working in the world of science-making, and the communal political, ecological, and territorial struggles that intellectual inquiry often contends with.

We understand friction as the force between two objects in contact with each other, as well as a form of disagreement and tension between different interests and groups. While friction is a form resistance to motion, it can also be what makes traction and movement possible. The symposium as such is placed at the point of friction between scientific commitments—especially those of the disciplines of the arts, architecture, ecology, and geography—and the political, territorial, and ecological struggles ‘we’ find ourselves embedded in, or in contact with. So doing we aim to not begin from (assumed) shared premises, but instead from the dissonances, clashes, and contestation between oftentimes opposing and not necessarily aligned agendas, and ends, of a multitude of actors.

With a primary focus on methodological questions—broadly conceived as the ‘ways of working’—the symposium is organized in a series of workshops, seminars, lectures, and activities. Doing so allows us to engage with concrete struggles, the ethical and philosophical implications that arise when science-making faces communal struggle, and the personal histories and lived experiences that each of us holds. At the same time, and in extension of the former, we also understand that methodological considerations prefigure, and lie within, the ethical undergirdings that guide our work and lives.

This symposium is organized between ARQDIS at Universidad de Los Andes and the Manchester School of Architecture. Set participants include academics and post-graduate students from Universidad de Los Andes, the University of Salford, and the Manchester School of Architecture. But, we welcome and encourage applications from anybody interested and invested in the questions we set out to explore and would be more than happy to facilitate participation. To contribute with a paper, poetry, audio-visual artwork, shortfilms, or any other form of intervention, please do submit an abstract and a short bio to the call below.
